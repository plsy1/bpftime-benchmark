# E5-real: field-level audit on real applications (x86 NUC, 2026-11-27)

Predictions: e5/PREDICTIONS-v5.md (sha256 b6105afe…, recorded 13:15:00 before any E5-real code was written or run).
Code: e5/real (src/e5real_k.bpf.c, src/e5real_u.bpf.c, src/e5rtool.c, java/E5Threads.java, run_e5real.py,
compare_e5real.py). Hashes: binaries in src/sha256.txt (the .c sources are not hashed there); the runner and comparer as
run in results/E5real-20261127-try1.code-sha256 (= run_e5real.py.pre-hangdiag). Rows: results/E5real-20261127-try1.jsonl;
per-item directories runs/E5real-20261127-try1/.

## Method

- **Truth:** the kernel tracepoint sys_enter_write, restricted to the workload's own cgroup v2.
- **Probe:** a uprobe on libc `write`, in one of two forms:
  - bpftime legs: every process that loads the agent via LD_PRELOAD;
  - control leg Kc: the kernel uprobe.
- **Pairing:** same (fd, buf, count), truth time inside a window after the probe time, earliest unused truth record.
  No identity field is used.
- **Fields compared:** tgid, tid, uid, gid, comm.
- **Deviations from the eval plan:**
  - The plan used K and Tern on two different functions and paired them by tid + time. E5-real uses the tracepoint as
    truth, puts both probes on the same function, pairs on data instead of tid, and adds Kc as a pairing control.
  - Reason: tid is itself one of the audited fields.
- **Deviations from v5, neither of which changed any result:**
  - The comparer's window is [t − 50 µs, t + 20 ms] instead of v5's [t, t + 20 ms]. Across all 409 336 pairs,
    t_truth − t_probe is +181 ns … +736 µs, never negative, so the slack was never used. Re-pairing with v5's window
    gives identical counts (verifier).
  - Ambiguity was tested over unused candidates only. Counting used candidates too, no window ever held truth records
    from two different threads.
- **Workloads:**
  - N: Debian nginx 1.26.3, master as root, 1 worker as nobody, 2000 HTTPS requests from curl outside the cgroup.
    All 2000 returned 200 in every item.
  - M: `make -j4` of libbpf's src/, static library only. 74–76 processes per item write.
  - J: a small OpenJDK 21 program written for this test (not a third-party application): 4 named threads, one of which
    renames itself halfway.
- **Legs:** Kc, O, Ol, Dn, Dnpc, Dxn+EXACT, Dnextpc, Dnextpc+CAREFUL. 3 runs each (index 0–2); leg order shuffled per
  run and workload; 72 items.
- **SPDLOG_LEVEL=off for the workload processes (set after the smoke):** the agent's own start-up log lines are
  write(2)s that exist only because of the agent. With them on, there were about 13 unpaired truth records per process.

**What is wrong.** The wrong field is always comm. tgid, tid, uid and gid are right on all 409 336 pairs.

**N (nginx).**
- The worker's writes carry truth uid/gid 65534: 12 045 of 12 075 records over 3 runs; the other 30 are the master's,
  uid 0.
- Every leg reports them right. This is fork plus glibc setuid/setgid, the W1/W7g pattern.

**M (make), wrong on O, Ol, Dn, Dnpc and Dnextpc for every record of these processes:**
- as (2 217–2 220 records per leg over 3 runs) and ar (1 467–1 470): the comm is taken from the basename of the
  symlink target x86_64-linux-gnu-as/-ar.
  - Ol, Dn, Dnpc and Dnextpc report "x86_64-linux-gn": 15 bytes plus NUL, as the kernel would truncate.
  - O (official) reports all 16 bytes "x86_64-linux-gnu" with **no NUL terminator**. The kernel always terminates. This
    is a second deviation of the official build, not predicted.
- sh (75) reports "dash".
- make and cc1 are right: their exe basename is the comm.
- Writing processes affected: 47 of 74–76 per item (sh 25, as 21, ar 1), so 62–64 %, against 26 % of records. cc1
  makes 73 % of the records.

**J (JVM program).**
- Every record from the 4 named threads (e5-worker-1..4, which also writes as e5-renamed) reports "java".
- 4 of the 5 writing threads are affected; the main thread ("java") is right.
- 97.4 % is a property of the chosen line counts, not of JVMs.

**Exact and careful modes.** Dxn+EXACT and Dnextpc+CAREFUL have 0 wrong fields anywhere.

**Control.** Kc has 0 wrong fields and 100 % pairs: the pairing works.

**Coverage, M.**
- Kc: 100 %.
- Ol, Dn, Dnpc, Dxn, Dnextpc and CAREFUL: 99.96 %. Two writes per item are in truth but not seen by the probe:
  - pkg-config's 1-byte stdout write from `$(shell pkg-config …)`;
  - cc's 17-byte "x86_64-linux-gnu\n" from `$(shell cc -dumpmachine)`.
  - Likely exit-time stdio flushes after the agent's hooks are gone; not shown.
- O: these two writes **do not occur at all**. They are absent from the kernel truth in all O items (3 main, 6
  repeats, smoke), and O items have 74 instead of 76 writing processes. The program output was lost under the
  official agent. The build still succeeded. Cause not investigated.

**Coverage, N and J.** 100 % on every leg.

**Workloads per leg.** They differ slightly but deterministically: as 739 vs 740 writes per item, ar 489 vs 490, make
27–28. This is likely the leg name in the build path ending up in the objects.

## Against PREDICTIONS-v5

Confirmed wherever observable:
- N "-" on every leg.
- M: comm wrong on sh, as and ar; right on make and cc1.
- J: comm wrong on every named-thread record, right on main.
- No pid/tid/uid/gid error.
- Kc clean.
- The JVM did not crash under any agent.

Not as predicted, or not testable:
- **Predicted value.** v5 named "x86_64-linux-gn" for every off leg; O reports the unterminated 16-byte form.
- **cc.** The predicted cc mismatch is untestable with a write() probe in this build. The 21 compiling cc drivers never
  write. The only cc write is the start-up `cc -dumpmachine`, which O does not produce and the other legs do not see.
- **pkg-config** (→ pkgconf; would be a comm error) is not in v5. Its only write is unseen, as above.

## Hang (not predicted)

**The hung item, M/Dnextpc/run index 1:**
- workload.log ends at "AR …/libbpf.a", without make's "Leaving directory" line, which every other M item has.
- make's last write, 3.171 s after the item's first truth record, is a 1-byte jobserver token on fd 8.
- The libbpf.pc sed step never wrote its 251 bytes. That write is present in every other main-run and repeat item.
- 75 writing processes instead of 76.
- 3 processes were left in the cgroup at the 254 s limit, when it was killed.

**What the evidence points to:** make waiting on the sh/sed job of the libbpf.pc rule, which plausibly accounts for
the 3 survivors. No stacks were captured, because the runner had no hang capture then.

**Repeats.** Afterwards the runner captures cgroup tasks, kernel stacks and gdb backtraces on a timeout. That capture
code is untested: it never triggered. Repeats with it, all ok, no hang:
- 30 items: M × (Dnextpc, Dn, Dnpc, O, Dnextpc+CAREFUL) × 6;
- 40 items: M × (Dnextpc, Dn) × 20.

**Rate:**

**Status.** Observed on the Dnextpc build; branch of origin and mechanism unknown. The inherited-lock fork hang

Recorded, not fixed.

## Reading

- **nginx, de-privileged worker:** every build is exact.
- **One real build** (libbpf, `make -j4`, write() records), on every comm-caching build, upstream and Tern alike
  (O/Ol/Dn/Dnpc/Dnextpc): 26 % of records and about 63 % of writing processes carry a wrong comm. The cause is
  symlinked or long-named executables (dash, binutils). The record share depends on the build: cc1 dominates the
  writes.
- **JVM program:** every record from a named Java thread is wrong (4 of 5 writing threads).
- **pid/tid/uid/gid:** no error in any of the three workloads.
- **Careful mode** (per-thread PR_GET_NAME) **and exact mode** remove every comm error. This is the real-application
  case for them.
- **Side findings on the official build:**
  - an unterminated 16-byte comm;
  - lost short-lived $(shell) output.
- **Scope:** uprobe-only mode on x86, like all E5 runs.
