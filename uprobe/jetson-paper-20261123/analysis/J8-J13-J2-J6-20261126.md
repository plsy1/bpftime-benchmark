| 2048 | 2115 | 4279 | 4299 | 698 | 1.35 | 2.33 |
| 4096 | 2398 | 4802 | 4802 | 834 | 1.53 | 2.78 |

- **No flat region on Orin.** Unlike x86 (flat to 1 MB, then +45-70 % past its 1.25 MB L2), pin rises from the first step,
  +10 % at 128 KB, and keeps rising.
- **Knees:**
  - The L2 knee (256 KB) shows in getpid: its largest step is 256 -> 512 KB, from 1.37x to 2.05x.
  - Pin has no sharp L2 step: +7 % from 256 to 512 KB.
  - Its largest step is 2 -> 4 MB, +13 %, past the 2 MB L3.
- **Absolute increase:**
  - At 1 MB, pin rises by about +450 ns and getpid by about +390 ns.
  - At 4 MB, pin rises by about +830 ns and getpid by about +530 ns.
  - So the cache effect is roughly a per-kernel-entry cost. It is not specific to the affinity path: pin makes 3 syscalls and gains about as much as one getpid.
- **Masks:**
  - pair ≈ all ≈ 2.0x single at every ws. Restoring a 2+-CPU mask costs about as much again as the single-CPU pin.
  - The ratio does not depend on the working set.
- **v1 cross-check:** v1 (difference method) agrees at ws 0 (1572 vs 1570) but is noisy above L2 (v1 2 MB: 2400 vs v2 2115).
- **Q6:** the in-situ pin premium on Orin is 2.1-2.4x. Working-set size alone gives at most 1.53x at 4 MB.
  - pair/single is 2.0x, and nginx runs under a 2-CPU mask (cores 4-5, policy M).
  - So whether the "idle" reference was taken under a single-CPU or a multi-CPU mask may matter more than the cache. Not checked here.

## AOT code-model note (NUC R-PR, 11-26): no Orin run needed

NUC found the x86 cause of Tern's slower probe read: AOT objects are compiled with CodeModel::Large, so helper calls
become indirect calls. On the P-cores an indirect call + ret costs about 4 cycles against about 1 for a direct one.

On Orin this is already covered:
- D (9ccda7e8) contains the AOT cache commits (f37c9502, a73218ad).
- The cache is on unless BPFTIME_AOT_CACHE=0, and scripts/run-e3.sh never sets it.
- The same E3 harness gave x86 D 2,533 vs O 1,694 (the AOT penalty), while Orin's E3 gave D/O probe read 1.002 and
  write 1.003.

So aarch64 shows no penalty. On aarch64 a large-model call is `blr` and the return still uses the return-address stack.
The BPFTIME_AOT_CACHE=0 rerun NUC suggested (optional) was skipped.
