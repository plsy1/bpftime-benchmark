  (paired 0.68 us/event [0.03, 1.34]), 0.4 % on counting legs. x86: 1.0037, equivalent.
- With checks, Dnpc is 3.2-3.3 % slower than Dpc in both leg types (per round 1.6-5 %; paired
  1.21 us/event [0.24, 2.18]). x86: 1.0011, equivalent. So the "old-dev numbers carry over"
  statement holds on x86 but not for the checked build on Orin.
- The 2.2 % Dx/Dpc gap of E16 is not reproduced on the new base: Dxn equals Dnpc on stock legs
  and is 2.5 % faster on counting legs. Both new checked builds (Dnpc, Dxn) sit 3 % below old Dpc;
  Dpc-stock is 14,240 rps in both E16 and E18, and every other checked build (Dx 13,926, Dnpc
  13,748, Dxn 13,832) is lower. No cause attributed.
- The recorded cmake options of Dpc and Dnpc differ only in options that exist in one source tree
  and not the other (`BPFTIME_BUILD_EXECUTABLE`, `BPFTIME_BUILD_KERNEL_BPF`, `BPFTIME_ENABLE_CCACHE`,
  `BPFTIME_VERBOSE_OUTPUT` old; `BPFTIME_ENABLE_FUZZING` new); D and Dn differ the same way.
  Probe checks, LTO, build type, compilers and flags are identical.
- Excluding the rows that overlapped a tool call at 19:55 (round 0 rep 6-7) changes no ratio by
  more than 0.6 % and no verdict.
